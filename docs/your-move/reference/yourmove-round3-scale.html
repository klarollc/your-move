  • A1One ordering rule per group, no exceptions. Your move: longest waiting first (oldest need gets the top). In progress: most recent signal first (what's alive). Queued: oldest first (FIFO). Done: most recently verified first. Deterministic, explainable, no "relevance" black box.
  • A2Collapsed groups name their head item. "31 items — most recent: …" means a collapsed group still tells you something true instead of being a bare number.
  • A3Wait durations in plain words. "Waiting 3d" next to the revision — the manager reads the urgency; the revision carries the proof.

  • B1No sampling. The rail scrolls through all 11 rather than showing a "top 5." A "top N" rail would be the UI deciding which of your obligations you see — exactly the kind of quiet lying the product exists to avoid.
  • B2The overflow line counts, it doesn't tease. "+ 4 more" plus a link that filters the list to your-move. The count is the honesty; the link is the convenience.
  • B3Oldest first is a fairness rule. The thing waiting 4 days sits above the thing waiting 2 hours, always. No engagement optimization, no recency bias.

  • C1Group counts go fractional. "(1 of 11)" — the group tells you both what the filter shows and what exists. Nothing about the filter is allowed to look like the world.
  • C2Rail labeled "unfiltered." One word that prevents the worst misreading: that your obligations shrank because your search did.
  • C3Filters are chips, not a panel. Visible, individually removable, one "clear all." The filter state is always legible at a glance.

Decisions for you

Rule on these

  • 1. The four ordering rules (your-move: longest waiting first; in progress: most recent signal; queued: oldest; done: most recently verified). Any objections?
  • 2. Rail at scale: full scroll, no sampling (mock B). Or would you rather cap it at 5 with "+N more"?
  • 3. Group collapse: auto-collapse past ~8 items with the head-item line (mock A), and should collapse state persist per session?
