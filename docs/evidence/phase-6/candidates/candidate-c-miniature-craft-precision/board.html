4 · Material samples

Nine materials, one family each, no second wood and no second metal. Each is drawn with the same three-face volume primitive, so material reads come from value separation and edge treatment rather than from bespoke per-object shading.

8 · Lighting and contact shadow

  • Key: fixed soft daylight, one direction, never status-dependent. A blocked office and a calm office are lit identically; severity is carried by the subject, not the room.
  • Practicals: warm lamp glow at radius 26px, subordinate to the key and confined to lamp objects.
  • Contact shadow: blur 1, opacity 0.34, spread 0.5. Every object that touches the floor gets one; nothing floats.
  • Plinth: 14px of visible floor thickness, containing the scene as an object.

9 · Prop density and negative space

Core props: 7 zones carry core and warm-density props. Warm-density props are drawn in this candidate.
  • No zone exceeds four objects, so every zone keeps a readable empty region.
  • Circulation between zones stays clear; no prop sits on a boundary line.
  • Props never encode state. A crate in Delivery does not mean work was delivered.
  • Density is declared here, not discovered by drawing — the same prop list drives all three candidates, filtered by one flag.

10 · Amber attention and focus

One accent, three permitted uses: manager attention, keyboard focus, primary action. It is not used for role identity, not for severity, and not for the five non-active agents. In the frozen frame exactly one subject carries it.

12 · Strengths, risks, exclusions, complexity, assets

Strengths

  • Material edges and contact shadows give the strongest sense of real, touchable surfaces.
  • The plinth contains the office as a single object, which makes the fixed camera feel deliberate rather than arbitrary.
  • Staged props read as considered rather than scattered; depth is legible at both viewports.
  • Highest value separation between materials, which helps contrast at small scale.

Named risk, and how this board addresses it

Reading as a toy, a diorama, or a game rather than a serious work environment.

  • Adult proportions and work-realistic props keep it away from toy scale; nothing is stylised small.
  • The plinth is flat and unlit rather than a display base, which is what would push it toward diorama.
  • Muted palette and absence of ambient motion keep it from reading as a game scene.
  • Unresolved: the contact-shadow weight that makes materials convincing is also what most invites the miniature reading. It cannot be tuned out of the risk without losing the strength.

Exclusions

  • No high-fidelity texture, no photographic material, no bespoke per-object art. Those are Phase 7 and unauthorized.
  • No token promotion. Every value on this page is Candidate.
  • No claim about any viewport, browser or interaction not directly exercised here.

Implementation complexity and asset needs

  • Feasibility: every surface is the same three-face volume primitive with flat fills — expressible as PixiJS meshes or sprites without a custom shader. Lamp glow needs one additive sprite per practical. The plinth is two extra quads on the scene root.
  • Asset count: 12 distinct drawable kinds, before avatar variation.
  • DOM shell: all labels are real text and have accessible-outline equivalents; none is canvas-only.
  • Cost driver: prop variety — warm-density objects roughly double the staging pass.

Focus intent

A two-layer ring on the focused subject: an inner ring in the reserved accent and an outer ring in the scene ground, so the indicator holds against both the floor and any object it may sit on. Focus is never carried by fill alone, never by the absence of a ring on other subjects, and never shares its treatment with the attention marker — attention says the work needs you, focus says the keyboard is here.

1280 × 800 accommodation

  • Uniform scale-to-fit: the same scene, the same margins, a smaller scale factor. No reflow.
  • No zone, agent, label, prop or state marker is dropped, merged or abbreviated.
  • The camera does not move, rotate, or change its angle; reset view returns to the same position at both sizes.
  • Label plates keep their pixel size relative to the scene, so text scales with the composition rather than staying fixed and crowding it.
  • The accessible Office outline is unchanged at both viewports; it is not a function of scale.

Measured, not asserted: docs/evidence/phase-6/accessibility-report.json counts the rendered text in both compositions and the suite fails if the narrower viewport carries fewer labels.
