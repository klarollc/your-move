## Proposed reconciliation — requires owner approval before Task 9

**Retained from B (base):** warm palette and plaster values, textiles, the full warm-density prop
set, practical lamp lighting, and soft top-face lift. The candidate's emphasis — a place people work
rather than a diagram of one — is the thing being preserved.

**Imported from A (clarity), each named:**

1. **A defining edge on solid faces.** B draws no outline at all, which is the single largest source
   of its softness. A uses 1.1px. The reconciliation proposes 0.7px — enough to separate silhouettes
   without the diagrammatic reading that is A's own named risk.
2. **A grounded contact shadow.** B's shadow is blur 5 / spread 2, the softest of the three, and it
   lets objects read as hovering. Tightened toward A's 1.5 / 1 without going flat.
3. **Stronger floor-zone separation.** Zone boundaries are the primary clarity signal in A and the
   weakest channel in B. The reconciliation widens the value gap between adjacent zones and darkens
   the zone edge stroke.

**Deliberately not imported:** A's restraint on props. Removing B's warm-density props would remove
the warmth that made it the strongest candidate; the clarity problem is edge and zoning definition,
not object count. The Visual QA Reviewer's noise concern is answered by definition rather than by
subtraction, and that choice is the owner's to overturn.

**Constraint on the pass:** every contrast and accessibility result established in Tasks 2–6 must
still hold. Edge, shadow and floor-value changes all move measured contrast, so the reconciliation is
re-measured rather than assumed, and no result may regress.
